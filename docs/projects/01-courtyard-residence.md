## Narrative

### The idea
The Courtyard Residence is conceived for a multi-generational Riyadh family who host often and live privately. FurnishIQ organises the 3,800 m² home into three wings — a formal majlis wing, a family wing and a guest wing — all opening onto a central courtyard with a long reflecting pool. The courtyard is the home's quiet centre: every principal room looks onto it, and every route through the house passes it.

### Hospitality, privately
The majlis wing is designed for receiving guests at scale without compromising family privacy. The 220 m² grand majlis rises to a double height, wrapped in honed travertine and fluted walnut, with walnut mashrabiya screens that temper the afternoon sun and cast a moving pattern across the floor. A separate women's majlis and a formal dining room for twenty-four complete the guest sequence, served by a discreet catering kitchen with its own staff access.

### Materials with restraint
The palette is deliberately narrow: travertine, walnut, bronze and limestone, repeated across every wing so the house reads as one composition. Detail does the work that ornament would elsewhere — shadow gaps at every ceiling junction, bronze inlays set flush into stone, and joinery aligned to a single setting-out grid from floor to ceiling.

### Engineering that disappears
The MEP strategy is planned alongside the interior from the first sketch. Conditioned air is delivered through linear slot diffusers concealed in ceiling shadow gaps; acoustic separation isolates the majlis wing from the family wing; lighting, shading, climate and security run on a single KNX smart-home system, with scene control in every room. Service routes are coordinated in 3D before any ceiling closes, so access panels are minimal and never visible from the principal rooms.

### Furniture made for the house
FurnishIQ specifies and sources the furniture as part of the same design: low majlis seating in ivory bouclé on walnut plinths, a 24-seat dining table in book-matched walnut, and lounge pieces in cognac leather. Each piece is selected or made to order against the approved 3D visualisation, so the finished rooms match the design without substitution.

## FurnishIQ scope (as listed on the page)
- **Interior Design:** concept, spatial planning, photoreal 3D visualisation of every principal room, material and lighting design
- **Fit-Out:** stone, joinery, ceilings, flooring and finishes across all three levels
- **MEP Engineering:** HVAC, electrical, lighting control, plumbing, fire safety and KNX smart-home integration
- **Furniture:** specification, sourcing and installation of furniture, rugs and decorative pieces
