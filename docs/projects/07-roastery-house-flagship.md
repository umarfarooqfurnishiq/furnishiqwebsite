## Narrative (project page, 6 paragraphs)
1. **Coffee as theatre.** Roastery House Flagship is a FurnishIQ design concept for a flagship roastery café in north Riyadh. The 1,200 m² space puts production on show: the roaster sits behind a glazed wall at the centre of a double-height hall, so every guest sees the coffee they drink being roasted.
2. **The espresso bar.** A 14-metre bar in travertine and aged brass runs the length of the hall, with an espresso station at one end and a slow-bar for filter coffee at the other. Behind the bar, a wall of smoked oak shelving holds the retail range and the cups and equipment in daily use.
3. **Materials of clay and roast.** The palette follows the coffee itself: handmade terracotta tile on the bar front and walls, clay-toned terrazzo floors, lime-washed plaster, smoked oak and aged brass. Cognac leather banquettes and oak tables give the seating the comfort of a lounge rather than a quick-service café.
4. **Upstairs.** A mezzanine lounge overlooks the roasting hall through a bronze balustrade, with quieter seating, a cupping lab for tastings and a training room for baristas. The green-bean store sits behind the roaster, visible through a second glazed wall.
5. **Engineering a working roastery.** A roaster inside a café demands careful engineering. FurnishIQ plans dedicated roaster exhaust with afterburner treatment, make-up air and pressure control so roasting smoke never reaches the seating, heavy-duty power and gas services, acoustic treatment for the grinders and roaster, and water filtration for every espresso and brewing station.
6. **Furniture for long visits.** Furniture is designed for guests who stay: cognac leather banquettes, smoked oak tables with brass edges, terracotta-topped side tables and bar stools in oak and leather. FurnishIQ's scope covers the complete design-build — interior design and photoreal 3D visualisation, fit-out, MEP engineering, and furniture specification, sourcing and installation.
