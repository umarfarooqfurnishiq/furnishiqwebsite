## Narrative (project page, 6 paragraphs)
1. **A showroom planned as a house.** The Furniture Gallery is a FurnishIQ design concept for a two-level furniture showroom in north Riyadh. Instead of open retail floors, the 4,000 m² plan is a sequence of rooms — living, dining, bedroom and study — so clients see each piece in the setting it is made for.
2. **The atrium.** Clients enter a double-height atrium lit from above by a long skylight. A sculptural stair in white oak and plaster rises through it to the upper galleries, and the first room-sets open off the atrium on both levels.
3. **Rooms, not aisles.** Each room-set is built as a real interior, with its own walls, ceiling, lighting and flooring. Ivory lime plaster, white oak, travertine and aged brass form a quiet background that lets the furniture change season by season without rebuilding the rooms.
4. **The design studio.** On the upper level, a client design studio and material library hold fabric, leather, timber, stone and metal samples alongside large worktables. Here FurnishIQ designers plan complete homes with clients, from a single room to a full villa.
5. **Engineering for display.** A showroom lives on its lighting. FurnishIQ plans a flexible track and recessed lighting grid with high colour rendering, daylight control under the skylight, zoned air conditioning for each gallery, concealed power in the floors for lamps and displays, and a back-of-house loading and assembly area connected by a goods lift.
6. **Furniture as the exhibit.** Display platforms, wall systems, the stair and the studio worktables are part of the same design, made in white oak, plaster and brass. FurnishIQ's scope covers the complete design-build — interior design and photoreal 3D visualisation, fit-out across both floors, MEP engineering, and display furniture and joinery.
