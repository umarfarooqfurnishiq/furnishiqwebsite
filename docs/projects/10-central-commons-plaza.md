## Narrative (project page, 6 paragraphs)
1. **A square for the city.** Central Commons is a FurnishIQ design concept for the public spaces of a mixed-use development in Olaya, in the centre of Riyadh. FurnishIQ's 22,000 m² scope covers everything a visitor walks through: a shaded central square, a covered retail galleria, a central atrium, a food hall and the lobbies of the office towers above.
2. **Shade first.** The square is designed for Riyadh's climate. A tall canopy of perforated bronze-anodised shading filters the sun into a soft dappled light, linear water features and planted courtyards cool the air, and limestone paving stays pale and comfortable underfoot. In the evening the canopy is lit from below and the square becomes a place to meet.
3. **The galleria and atrium.** From the square, a covered galleria runs through the retail levels to a central atrium under a long skylight. Shopfronts follow FurnishIQ design guidelines — bronze frames, limestone surrounds and a consistent signage zone — so that each brand has its own character while the galleria reads as one calm space.
4. **The food hall.** A food hall for 600 guests sits on the upper retail level, with an open seating hall in oak and terrazzo, communal tables and quieter booths, and kitchens planned around a single service spine. Large windows look out over the square.
5. **Engineering a public building.** Public spaces of this scale carry heavy engineering. FurnishIQ plans displacement cooling in the galleria and atrium, misting and air movement in the shaded square, smoke management for the atrium, kitchen extract and grease management for the food hall, wayfinding and lighting control across the whole public realm, and accessible routes throughout.
6. **Furniture for a public place.** Benches, planters, kiosks, seating and lighting are designed as one family in limestone, oak and bronze, built to last in heavy public use. FurnishIQ's scope covers the complete design-build of the public realm — interior design and photoreal 3D visualisation, fit-out, MEP engineering, shopfront guidelines, and furniture specification, sourcing and installation.
